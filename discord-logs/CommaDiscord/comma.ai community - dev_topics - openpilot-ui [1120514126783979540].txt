@cXk , @First .

Here are the user stories as we discussed.

As a driver, I want a lane change indicator feature in my vehicle that provides me with real-time information about the safety of changing lanes. This feature should display one of two statuses:

• Safe to Change: When it is safe to change lanes, the indicator will be green, indicating that there are no obstacles or vehicles in the intended lane.
• Blocked: When it is not safe to change lanes, the indicator will be red w/ icon, alerting me that there are obstacles or vehicles preventing a safe lane change. Icon: https://www.iso.org/obp/ui#iso:grs:7000:2796

With this lane change indicator, I can make more informed decisions while driving and ensure a safer and smoother lane-changing experience.

[6/20/2023 1:10 AM] nyhunk
Outlining the ideas being explored here, feedback is much welcomed

1. Replacing edge lines with yellow: The purpose of using yellow edge lines could be to make them more visually distinct and aligned with what drivers typically encounter on the roads. This can help the comma users better understand the boundaries of the lane. However, it's important to consider local regulations and guidelines for road markings. Different regions may have specific color coding standards for lane markings, so it would be necessary to adhere to those guidelines.
2. Replacing solid lines with dotted lines or reflective markings: This idea aims to improve the relationship between what the driver perceives and what the driving system recognizes. Dotted lines or reflective markings are commonly used on roads to indicate temporary or advisory boundaries. Implementing such markings could provide additional cues to the driving system, enhancing its ability to understand lane positioning and changes.
3. Dividing lane lines into three segments: This suggestion aims to help users differentiate safe distances and understand when the driving system, like Comma, might slow down or speed up. Breaking down the lane lines into segments could provide visual indicators of safe following distances, similar to adaptive cruise control systems. This can be useful for assisting drivers in maintaining safe distances from the vehicles ahead and optimizing the behavior of the driving system accordingly.


[6/20/2023 1:14 AM] frogsgomoo
That’s how I have the lane detection logic setup in mine. It doesn’t prevent lane changes, just prevents nudgless lane changes.


[6/20/2023 1:16 AM] nyhunk
4. Arrows: Displaying arrows indicating the intended direction of the lane change can give drivers a clear visual indication of the upcoming maneuver. For example, if the vehicle intends to move to the left lane, an arrow pointing left could be shown. 
5. Lane direction cues: Providing visual cues that show the direction of the lanes can aid drivers in understanding the lane layout. For instance, using dashed lines or chevron symbols that depict the flow of traffic in each lane can be beneficial. This helps drivers anticipate and prepare for lane changes and reduces confusion.
6. Use a clean and uncluttered design: Opt for a minimalist design approach that emphasizes simplicity and clarity. Avoid excessive visual elements, distracting animations, or unnecessary graphics. Keep the UI layout clean and organized, ensuring that information is presented in a logical and easy-to-read manner.
7. Context-aware information display: Tailor the displayed information based on the current driving situation. For example, when approaching an upcoming intersection, highlight relevant navigation instructions or system errors. Contextual awareness allows for dynamic adjustment of the UI to provide the most relevant and timely information.
